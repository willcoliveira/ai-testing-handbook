## practice: prompt-injection
- **arXiv, 2026-11:** a taxonomy of nine dimensions along which prompt injection attacks differ, applied to nine prompt injection benchmarks, finds that most cover only a small region of that space; combining benchmarks helps, but 13% of pairwise category combinations remain untested, mostly among the less commonly represented categories [S509].
- **arXiv, 2026-11:** a comparison of in-prompt defences with deterministic enforcement outside the model (four reference monitors of increasing strength), in an executable environment of 13 tasks and 12 tools, finds that two open-weights models (Qwen2.5-1.5B and Qwen2.5-7B, 156 attack episodes per defence and model) proposed the attacker's call in 64% and 84% of attack episodes behind the task-bound monitor, which admitted 1.3% and 7.7%, while guardrail effects depended on the model (a prompt guardrail moved obedience from 67% to 65% and from 86% to 55%, spotlighting plus a detector to 12% and 0%) against static attacks; the authors say the real-model evidence is limited to two open-weights models and static attacks; separately, the abstract reports that a naive monitor admits 18 of 33 effective attack variants, a hardened one 6 and a capability-bound one 1 [S514].
- **arXiv, 2026-11:** enforcement defenses against prompt injection ask the agent's own model to write a security artifact that code then enforces, and across 15 open-weight models, 13 of them between 1.7B and 12B parameters, the policies, graphs and constraints that the agent's model writes admit attacks on 14 (Progent), 13 (IPIGuard) and 11 (DRIFT) of the models, and CaMeL's programs fail to run in up to 68% of episodes; the authors' CapLite derives its policy from the tool schemas and admits a write only if every argument that decides whom or what it affects is stated in the user's request, and they report "No attack succeeds in 13,935 attacked AgentDojo episodes" and "utility under attack comparable to CaMeL's (29.0% vs. 27.2%)" [S521].
## practice: judge-calibration
- **arXiv, 2026-11:** in a study of psychological depth in short stories, with 60 blinded pairwise evaluations of prompt-matched outputs from GPT-5 against GPT-4o and DeepSeek-R1 against DeepSeek-V3 (the authors' "closest available contrast for reasoning-oriented post-training"), an ensemble LLM judge showed a strong pro-reasoning bias (98.3% of pairs against 55.2% of human votes), consistent across its five configurations and partly tracking sentence length; the seven human annotators showed no universal reasoning advantage (GPT-5 slightly preferred over GPT-4o at 60.0-62.9%, DeepSeek-R1 behind DeepSeek-V3 at 42.9%) with low agreement (Krippendorff's α = 0.070), which the authors' annotation-quality checks suggested was genuine evaluative subjectivity [S510].
## practice: rubrics-and-pairwise
- **arXiv, 2026-11:** DeepResearch RewardBench, a benchmark for evaluating reward modeling for research reports, collects report candidates from multiple agents or tool-augmented LLMs, has them scored using query-specific golden rubrics, and formats preference datasets as pairs, triplets and quadruplets; the authors report that while current reward models handle general coherence effectively, their performance significantly degrades when evaluating research reports, and that generative LLMs acting as judges "demonstrate comprehensive superiority in this domain, with comparative evaluation proving more effective than direct scoring methods"; the abstract gives no figures [S511].
## practice: post-training-evals
- **arXiv, 2026-11:** studying how best to use monitors to mitigate reward hacking during RL, where training against a monitor could lead to evasive behavior, the authors open source three environments where Qwen3-4B reward hacks and find in the coding one (hackable via test overwriting) that models can learn to evade highly accurate monitors by exploiting systemic flaws in probes and LLM judges, that monitors which leak more learning signal during RL suppress reward hacking but are more often evaded, and that including easier problems in training can decrease reward hacking; monitors built from these findings for the other two environments reduce reward hacking rates across seeds to before-training levels in some interventions; the abstract gives no figures [S512].
## practice: benchmark-hygiene
- **arXiv, 2026-11:** a technical report auditing how test observations support patch-correctness claims across classical repair systems and coding agents assembles 39 benchmark and evaluation artifacts and codes its 33 primary contributions along five dimensions (test provenance and exposure, behavioral scope, oracle basis and dependence, execution integrity, label adjudication); its central distinction separates checks visible during repair search from evidence reserved for final assessment, and four worked comparative audits show how changed candidate populations, oracle rules or execution budgets alter reported detection rates [S513].
## practice: harnesses
- **arXiv, 2026-11:** on AgentScript-Eval, an execution-graded benchmark of 100 real tasks for one enterprise DSL, the best of five models evaluated zero-shot reaches 25.0% pass@3 and every model scores 0% on authoring from scratch; the abstract then says "Adding an agentic compile-and-edit loop is the single largest lever, roughly doubling partial-credit accuracy", that "the wording of domain guidance matters more than its packaging", and that the same skill pack scores 28% as plain documentation but 14% as a live plugin and 37% after one skill inside that plugin is rewritten (it does not name the metric of these three scores), and it concludes "Larger models did not reliably outperform smaller ones; what mattered was the harness surrounding the model and the guidance supplied to it" [S515].
- **Inspect AI, 2026-11:** version 0.3.288 lists "remove deprecated web browser tools" under Features, and its Breaking Changes section says to replace calls to `web_browser()` or its `web_browser_tools()` alias with `web_search()` for retrieval, or `computer()` or sandbox MCP tools for browser interaction; the same section says evaluations using the automatically generated Docker sandbox must provide an explicit image if they need the `nonroot` account, `pipx` or Playwright browser dependencies [S066].
## practice: red-teaming
- **arXiv, 2026-11:** the abstract starts from the view that "As of Fall 2026, human red-teaming may be nearing saturation, especially on the Gemini family of models" and argues that "despite this apparent plateau, prompt injection attacks are still possible"; the authors design a synthetic evaluation dataset which uses random non-malicious actions as adversarial goals, and report that an agentic attacker of increased capabilities can reliably trick models into following instructions in tool output in their evaluation, even for models previously considered robust on public prompt injection benchmarks such as GraySwan Arena, which they say shows that current frontier defenses may require proactive, automated red-teaming; the abstract gives no attack success figures and does not name the models tested [S516].
- **Google DeepMind, 2026-11:** the Gemini 4 Carbon availability post reports that in simulated cyberattack testing environments Carbon blocked 99% of attack trajectories from Google's adaptive red-teaming agent, that the Carbon safeguards lowered the per-attempt breach rate to 0.8% from 3.1% on Argon (the post does not define a breach), that the automated cyberattack query success rate fell to 2.17% in Gray Swan's testing against 9.86% on Argon, and that external testing by Trajectory Labs found zero new universal jailbreaks across 132 hours and over 13,000 requests; the post adds that prompt injection robustness "remains an area for continued improvement" [S518].
## practice: capability-benchmarks
- **Google DeepMind, 2026-11:** the Multimodal Knowledge Agent (MKA) benchmark, launched with Artificial Analysis, has a 200-task evaluation set created by domain experts across 10 verticals and 83 occupations, each task with an expert-written rubric of roughly 70 yes/no checks weighted 75% on factual accuracy and 25% on visual design; an agentic autorater built on a non-Google model grades each deliverable, and domain experts who graded model outputs themselves agreed with it 96% of the time (the post does not say on how many outputs); Artificial Analysis runs the set and keeps it private, and every model runs in the same open-source harness with the same tools, sandbox and turn budget [S517].
## practice: mutation-checking
- **Google DeepMind, 2026-11:** to confirm that AlphaProof Code, its agentic framework for formal verification, "can tell correct code from buggy code", DeepMind reintroduced two real, previously fixed bugs into an isolated version of Signal's code, and in both cases the system produced a machine-checked proof that the buggy version was insecure; the agents generated over six million lines of Lean in a verification process that took four weeks, human experts reviewed every formal specification, and the agents audited the proof and highlighted one percent of the codebase as needing a second look by a human [S519].
