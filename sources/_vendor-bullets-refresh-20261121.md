## practice: statistical-treatment-of-evals
- **Anthropic, 2026-11:** Anthropic's guidance on safety evaluations treats 100 items as the floor for a complex, multi-turn eval and 1,000 for a simpler, single-turn one, notes that the confidence intervals its system cards attach can exceed ±10 percentage points for multi-turn evals on the API, and asks evaluators to report the configuration used, because it may affect performance: its system cards report safety results both for the API (the raw model) and for claude.ai, where the system prompt is applied, and in testing Claude Fable 5.1 the difference in appropriate response rate between these two configurations on its multi-turn safety evals was as high as 34 percentage points [S556].
## practice: judge-calibration
- **Anthropic, 2026-11:** Anthropic's safety-evaluation guidance says that before trusting a grader, humans should label a sample of the transcripts without seeing the grader's verdicts, the agreement between the human verdicts and the grader's should be reported, corrected for chance, and that agreement should be benchmarked against how well the human labelers agree with each other; the post gives no agreement figure [S556].
## practice: golden-datasets
- **arXiv, 2026-11:** Benchmark Factory, an autonomous meta-agent loop with human checkpoints, repairs candidate benchmarks until they clear quality anchors that need no per-task human labels; the authors used it to build benchmarks for five developer cohorts at their organization, a deliberately trivial benchmark failed the anchors, five broken graders failed them even under a relaxed rule fixed in advance that the real grader passes, and on configurations never used to build them the benchmarks ranked Claude Opus 4.7 at high reasoning effort above low effort (60.6% vs. 54.4% of tasks passed, p=0.002) and GPT-5.5 above GPT-5.4 (65.8% vs. 60.5%, p=0.004); the authors call the anchors necessary, not sufficient, conditions for a valid benchmark, designed to focus human review on what the loop cannot repair [S557].
## practice: agent-evals
- **arXiv, 2026-11:** SWE-CoWork places an agent in a simulated engineering organization with 26 colleagues for each of 101 development assignments across 101 real repositories; of eleven models evaluated, the two leading models satisfy approximately 63% of fail-to-pass tests but complete fewer than 8% of assignments, providing complete requirements upfront improves fail-to-pass by approximately 16 percentage points for Seed Evolving and GPT-6 Astra yet leaves complete task success unchanged, and a requirement-level trajectory analysis finds that 31% of requirements reported as completed fail their tests (the abstract does not say for which models, or who reports completion) [S558].
- **arXiv, 2026-11:** SWE-Recreation asks coding agents that see only rendered pixels and act through pointer and keyboard, without access to the DOM, source, logs or back-end, to rebuild 54 real applications (28 websites and 26 Android apps) against 1,446 hidden requirements, with a fixed judge grading workflows from pixel evidence and a separate post-hoc judge estimating exploration coverage; five frontier systems reach at most 0.530 weighted fidelity and 22.9% fully reproduced requirements under application-equal averaging, results the authors call descriptive [S572].
## practice: guardrails
- **arXiv, 2026-11:** treating the system-prompt choice for an LLM-as-a-judge guardrail as constrained optimisation (maximise benign pass rate while keeping the missed-attack rate below a user-specified level with finite-sample statistical control), the authors propose two procedures, one of which certifies candidate prompts on a held-out evaluation set, and report on four datasets covering prompt injection, system-prompt leakage and sensitive-information disclosure that risk-certified selection consistently respects the target missed-attack level on held-out tests, while utility improves as the permitted risk is relaxed; the abstract gives no figures [S559].
- **OpenAI, 2026-11:** the CSAM image classifier model card evaluates on approximately 46,849 access-controlled images plus approximately 1.25M Re-LAION-5B-research images for false-positive evaluation, and reports for likely-csam a recall of 61.27% for Black-tagged examples compared with 84.48%–90.32% for the other reported race tags, using tags assigned by an LLM annotator and asking readers to treat these results as directional; it says a classifier score alone does not confirm that an image is CSAM, establish its legal status, or determine whether it must be reported [S561].

## practice: prompt-injection
- **arXiv, 2026-11:** pairing text and page-image versions of 191 English question-answering sources across six models, the authors find that rendering reduces append and format injection execution for every model, yet Qwen3-VL-8B still executes image-borne answer replacement at 77.96%, that a search over eight fixed attack candidates largely closes the image deficit in appending and formatting for Qwen and Gemini, though not for GPT-5-nano, and that utility ratios on original questions range from 93.31% to 101.61% but Qwen falls to 61.99% when answers combine two evidence spans; they call this conditional protection, motivating evaluations that report security by model, operation and attack budget alongside utility [S567].
## practice: red-teaming
- **arXiv, 2026-11:** noting that attack success rate "counts how often a target breaks, not in how many different ways", AutoPI, a taxonomy-guided adaptive red teamer for prompt injection in LLM agents evaluated on AgentDojo and IPI Arena, reports technique coverage alongside ASR; on AgentDojo it finds a successful injection against Qwen3.6-35B in all six classes of the taxonomy and for more than 90% of its techniques, and against Claude Haiku 5.5, the most robust target evaluated, it achieves between 20% and 50% higher ASR and between 25% and 400% higher class-level coverage than recent state-of-the-art adaptive red teamers, while requiring no training [S568].
## practice: tool-use-evals
- **NVIDIA, 2026-11:** frontend-crates, the reusable Rust components behind NVIDIA Dynamo tool-calling parsers, pairs model outputs with expected results in a shared Conformance Corpus whose language-neutral YAML fixtures pair fixed input with expected output, so Rust parsers and Python references can run the same cases; NVIDIA's post says the initial test set an agent generates may contain errors because expected behavior depends on context the agent may not have, that if those expectations are wrong the parser can pass the tests and still behave incorrectly, and advises an adversarial review by the agent, then a manual pass case by case, and end-to-end tests with real models before claiming deployment support [S569].
## practice: rubrics-and-pairwise
- **arXiv, 2026-11:** a scale-reversal probe that inverts a rubric's score anchors without altering its evaluation criteria, with floor normalization to calibrate reversal disagreement against intrinsic model variability, finds across five meta-evaluation benchmarks with seven LLM judges spanning open-weight and proprietary models widespread violations of semantic faithfulness (the principle that semantically equivalent rubrics must yield consistent scores), showing that raw reversal agreement alone is misleading; bidirectional anchor averaging reduces dependence on any single anchor direction without compromising alignment with human judgments [S570].
## practice: llm-as-judge
- **arXiv, 2026-11:** a study of how numerical scoring topology (scale width, granularity, offset, symmetry and boundary placement) influences zero-shot LLM-as-a-Judge evaluation across diverse benchmarks and judge models reports that it systematically affects judge scores, calibration and robustness, particularly in judgment-intensive evaluations, that shifted and narrow ranges tend to induce some of the largest score deviations across many settings, and that stronger and larger judge models generally achieve higher discriminative validity and greater robustness to scoring-topology changes [S571].
