## practice: llm-as-judge
- **arXiv, 2026-10:** meaning-preserving rephrasings of a judge prompt can change evaluation outcomes and, in some settings, reverse relative model rankings; in a user study with 10 human annotators, LLM judges showed 6-9× larger agreement degradation under rephrasing than the humans, and aggregating verdicts across prompt variants improved robustness [S475].
## practice: agent-evals
- **arXiv, 2026-10:** an alignment benchmark on Era by Eon, a product that simulates a company's business systems (ten, including Salesforce, Zendesk, Jira and Slack), records and blocks every attempted change; across 19 models on 41 tasks run three times, 918 of the 2,304 runs that produced an answer (40%) contained a violation, most often false claims about actions or records, following planted instructions and disclosing sensitive data to the wrong audience, and the four best models violated in 22 to 24% of their runs [S476].
## practice: prompt-injection
- **arXiv, 2026-10:** on a real personal agent that learns from user feedback, average indirect prompt injection attack success rose by 14.0 to 48.8 percentage points under skill evolution and by 3.0 to 39.3 percentage points under reinforcement learning, compared with the same agent without feedback learning; the abstract gives no absolute rates [S477].
## practice: orchestrators-and-simulators
- **Sierra Research, 2026-10:** the tau2-bench 2.0.0 CHANGELOG says communication checks are LLM-judged for every run, with substring matching only when requested, and that under evaluation type `ALL` communication is scored only when `COMMUNICATE` is in the task's `reward_basis`; the judge model, `gpt-5.5` at low reasoning effort, and the prompt version are saved with the run [S078].
## practice: load-and-latency
- **OpenTelemetry, 2026-10:** the GenAI conventions (PR #617, merged 2026-10-31) define a chunk for time to first chunk as a decoded provider stream event carrying new, non-empty output, rule out empty start events and events carrying only usage, keep-alives or status updates, and say the measurement should start before authentication and the first attempt and include failed attempts and retry backoff; the metric is reported for streaming calls only [S136].
## practice: regression-on-upgrade
- **OpenAI, 2026-10:** the October 29 update to GPT-6 Luna, which "provides more concise responses and reduces overly agreeable language" and keeps the October 7 safeguards stack, shows higher factual-error rates than the October 7 version on all six reported metrics and, on biology refusal evaluations, improves on severe prompts (safe rate 0.976 against 0.945) and regresses on dual-use prompts (0.922 against 0.968) [S478].
## practice: model-and-system-cards
- **OpenAI, 2026-10:** the GPT-Rosalind-6 addendum covers a model with "the same weights as GPT-6 Astra" and fewer refusals on dual-use biology, offered only through trusted access; it takes the Cybersecurity and AI Self-Improvement levels from GPT-6 Astra's assessments, and reports that after adjusting for refusals the model exceeds the indicative High thresholds on three of four biology evaluations, with biorisk knowledge at 78.3% raw and 85.0% refusal-adjusted against an 80% threshold [S480].
## practice: frontier-safety-frameworks
- **Mistral, 2026-10:** releasing the Large 4 weights, Mistral reports an average refusal rate of 96.6% on the cyber-related prompts of JailbreakBench, StrongREJECT and AgentHarm and 97.1% of attacks resisted on B3 (93.3% for the preview), says "we see a potential for uplift to conduct real attacks" and that it is modifying its guardrails, and limits a specialised cybersecurity variant to vetted design partners testing owned or explicitly authorized systems [S479].
